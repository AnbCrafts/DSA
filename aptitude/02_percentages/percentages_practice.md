## ⚡ Quick Formula & Fraction-Percentage Table

### 1. Essential Fractions to Percentages
| Fraction | Percentage | Fraction | Percentage |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $1/2$ | $50\%$ | $1/7$ | $14.28\%$ ($14 \frac{2}{7}\%$) |
| $1/3$ | $33.33\%$ ($33 \frac{1}{3}\%$) | $1/8$ | $12.5\%$ ($12 \frac{1}{2}\%$) |
| $1/4$ | $25\%$ | $1/9$ | $11.11\%$ ($11 \frac{1}{9}\%$) |
| $1/5$ | $20\%$ | $1/10$ | $10\%$ |
| $1/6$ | $16.67\%$ ($16 \frac{2}{3}\%$) | $1/11$ | $9.09\%$ ($9 \frac{1}{11}\%$) |
| $1/12$ | $8.33\%$ ($8 \frac{1}{3}\%$) | $1/16$ | $6.25\%$ ($6 \frac{1}{4}\%$) |

### 2. Key Master Formulas
1. **Base Shift Rule**:
   - If $A$ is $x\% = \frac{a}{b}$ more than $B$, then $B$ is $\frac{a}{a+b} \times 100\%$ less than $A$.
   - If $A$ is $x\% = \frac{a}{b}$ less than $B$, then $B$ is $\frac{a}{b-a} \times 100\%$ more than $A$.
2. **Successive Percentage Change**:
   $$\text{Net Change} = \left(a + b + \frac{ab}{100}\right)\%$$
   *(Use positive for increase, negative for decrease/discount)*
3. **Product Constancy ($P \times C = E$)**:
   - If Price increases by $\frac{a}{b}$, consumption must decrease by $\frac{a}{a+b}$ to keep expenditure constant.
4. **Two-Set Venn Diagram**:
   $$n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$$
   $$\text{Neither} = 100\% - n(A \cup B)$$

## Type 1: Base Comparison, Fraction-Percentage & Error %

### Q1.
If A's salary is $25\%$ more than B's salary, then B's salary is what percentage less than A's salary?  
- **A)** $20\%$  
- **B)** $25\%$  
- **C)** $16.67\%$  
- **D)** $33.33\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> **Shortcut (Fraction Rule)**:  
> $+25\% = +\frac{1}{4} \implies$ Corresponding decrease is $-\frac{1}{4+1} = -\frac{1}{5} = \mathbf{20\%}$.  
> **Traditional**: Let $B = 100 \implies A = 125$.  
> Difference = $125 - 100 = 25$.  
> $\% \text{ less} = \left(\frac{25}{125}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$.

### Q2.
If the price of petrol increases by $20\%$, by what percentage must a car owner reduce fuel consumption so that the total monthly expenditure remains unchanged?  
- **A)** $20\%$  
- **B)** $16.67\%$  
- **C)** $15\%$  
- **D)** $25\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Price increased by $+20\% = +\frac{1}{5}$.  
> For constant expenditure, consumption reduction $= \frac{1}{5+1} = \frac{1}{6} = 16.67\% \text{ (or } 16\frac{2}{3}\%\text{)}$.

### Q3.
If $X$ is $20\%$ less than $Y$, then by what percentage is $Y$ more than $X$?  
- **A)** $20\%$  
- **B)** $22.5\%$  
- **C)** $25\%$  
- **D)** $30\%$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> **Fraction Rule**: $-20\% = -\frac{1}{5} \implies$ increase is $+\frac{1}{5-1} = +\frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{25\%}$.  
> **Traditional**: Let $Y = 100 \implies X = 80$.  
> $\% \text{ more} = \left(\frac{100 - 80}{80}\right) \times 100 = \frac{20}{80} \times 100 = \mathbf{25\%}$.

### Q4.
Two numbers are respectively $30\%$ and $40\%$ less than a third number. What percentage is the second number of the first number?  
- **A)** $85.71\%$  
- **B)** $75\%$  
- **C)** $80\%$  
- **D)** $90\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Let the third number be $100$.  
> First number $= 100 - 30 = 70$.  
> Second number $= 100 - 40 = 60$.  
> Second number as $\%$ of first $= \left(\frac{60}{70}\right) \times 100 = \frac{600}{7} = \mathbf{85.71\%} \text{ (or } 85\frac{5}{7}\%\text{)}$.

### Q5.
A student multiplied a number by $\frac{3}{5}$ instead of $\frac{5}{3}$. What is the percentage error in the calculation?  
- **A)** $36\%$  
- **B)** $44\%$  
- **C)** $64\%$  
- **D)** $56\%$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Let the number be $\text{LCM}(3, 5) = 15$.  
> Correct value $= 15 \times \frac{5}{3} = 25$.  
> Incorrect value $= 15 \times \frac{3}{5} = 9$.  
> Error $= 25 - 9 = 16$.  
> $\% \text{ Error} = \left(\frac{16}{25}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{64\%}$.

## Type 2: Successive Percentage Changes & Area/Volume Effects

### Q6.
The salary of an employee was first increased by $20\%$ and subsequently decreased by $20\%$. What is the net percentage change in his salary?  
- **A)** $0\%$ (No change)  
- **B)** $4\%$ increase  
- **C)** $4\%$ decrease  
- **D)** $2\%$ decrease  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Formula: $a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$  
> Net Change $= 20 - 20 + \frac{20 \times (-20)}{100} = 0 - 4 = \mathbf{-4\%}$ (i.e., **$4\%$ decrease**).

---

### Q7.
The length of a rectangle is increased by $30\%$ while its breadth is decreased by $20\%$. Find the net percentage change in the area of the rectangle.  
- **A)** $4\%$ increase  
- **B)** $4\%$ decrease  
- **C)** $10\%$ increase  
- **D)** $6\%$ decrease  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> $\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth}$.  
> Net Change $= 30 - 20 + \frac{30 \times (-20)}{100} = 10 - 6 = \mathbf{+4\%}$ (i.e., **$4\%$ increase**).

### Q8.
If the radius of a circle is increased by $25\%$, by what percentage does its area increase?  
- **A)** $50\%$  
- **B)** $56.25\%$  
- **C)** $62.5\%$  
- **D)** $45\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Area of circle $= \pi r^2$ (depends on $r \times r$, successive twice).  
> Net Change $= 25 + 25 + \frac{25 \times 25}{100} = 50 + 6.25 = \mathbf{56.25\%}$.

### Q9.
A retailer offers two successive discounts of $15\%$ and $10\%$ on an item. What single discount rate is equivalent to this offer?  
- **A)** $25\%$  
- **B)** $23.5\%$  
- **C)** $22.5\%$  
- **D)** $24\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Single Equivalent Discount $= d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}$  
> $= 15 + 10 - \frac{15 \times 10}{100} = 25 - 1.5 = \mathbf{23.5\%}$.

### Q10.
The price of a commodity is first increased by $10\%$, then decreased by $10\%$, and finally increased by $20\%$. What is the overall percentage change in the price?  
- **A)** $+18.8\%$  
- **B)** $+20\%$  
- **C)** $+19.2\%$  
- **D)** $+17.5\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Step 1: $10 - 10 - \frac{100}{100} = -1\%$.  
> Step 2: Combine $-1\%$ and $+20\%$:  
> $-1 + 20 + \frac{(-1)(20)}{100} = 19 - 0.2 = \mathbf{+18.8\%}$ (increase).  
> *(Or multiplying factors: $1.1 \times 0.9 \times 1.2 = 0.99 \times 1.2 = 1.188 \implies \mathbf{+18.8\%}$)*.

## Type 3: Expenditure, Price & Quantity Variations

### Q11.
The price of sugar rises by $25\%$. A household decides to increase its budget on sugar by only $10\%$. By what percentage must they reduce their sugar consumption?  
- **A)** $15\%$  
- **B)** $12\%$  
- **C)** $10\%$  
- **D)** $8\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Expenditure = Price $\times$ Consumption $\implies C = \frac{E}{P}$.  
> New Consumption factor $= \frac{1 + 0.10}{1 + 0.25} = \frac{1.10}{1.25} = \frac{110}{125} = \frac{22}{25} = 0.88 = 88\%$.  
> Reduction in consumption $= 100\% - 88\% = \mathbf{12\%}$.

### Q12.
A reduction of $20\%$ in the price of wheat enables a person to buy $5\text{ kg}$ more wheat for ₹$600$. Find the **original** price per kg of wheat.  
- **A)** ₹$24$  
- **B)** ₹$25$  
- **C)** ₹$30$  
- **D)** ₹$32$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Money saved by $20\%$ discount $= 20\% \text{ of } ₹600 = ₹120$.  
> This ₹$120$ buys the additional $5\text{ kg}$.  
> $\implies \text{Reduced price per kg} = \frac{120}{5} = ₹24/\text{kg}$.  
> Since reduced price is $80\%$ of original:  
> $\text{Original price} = \frac{24}{0.80} = \mathbf{₹30/\text{kg}}$.

### Q13.
The price of rice increases by $30\%$, causing a family to reduce its monthly consumption by $6\text{ kg}$, while their monthly expenditure increases by only $10\%$. Find the **original** consumption of rice.  
- **A)** $39\text{ kg}$  
- **B)** $45\text{ kg}$  
- **C)** $36\text{ kg}$  
- **D)** $42\text{ kg}$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Ratio of new consumption to old: $\frac{C_{\text{new}}}{C_{\text{old}}} = \frac{1.10}{1.30} = \frac{11}{13}$.  
> Decrease in units $= 13 - 11 = 2 \text{ units}$.  
> $2 \text{ units} = 6\text{ kg} \implies 1 \text{ unit} = 3\text{ kg}$.  
> Original consumption $= 13 \text{ units} = 13 \times 3 = \mathbf{39\text{ kg}}$.

### Q14.
An increase of $25\%$ in the price of apples enables a customer to buy $2$ apples less for ₹$240$. What is the **increased** price per apple?  
- **A)** ₹$24$  
- **B)** ₹$28$  
- **C)** ₹$30$  
- **D)** ₹$32$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Extra cost on ₹$240 = 25\% \text{ of } 240 = ₹60$.  
> Due to this ₹$60$, the person gets $2$ fewer apples.  
> $\implies \text{Increased (new) price per apple} = \frac{60}{2} = \mathbf{₹30}$.  
> *(Original price was $30 / 1.25 = ₹24$)*.

### Q15.
The price of diesel increases by $28\%$. A driver wants to increase his fuel expenditure by only $12\%$. By what percentage must he reduce his consumption?  
- **A)** $12.5\%$  
- **B)** $14.28\%$  
- **C)** $16\%$  
- **D)** $11.11\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**

> Reduction $= 1 - \frac{7}{8} = \frac{1}{8} = \mathbf{12.5\%}$.

## Type 4: Population, Depreciation & Multi-Year Growth

### Q16.
The population of a city is $1,20,000$. It increases by $10\%$ in the first year and by $20\%$ in the second year. What will be the population after $2$ years?  
- **A)** $1,56,000$  
- **B)** $1,58,400$  
- **C)** $1,60,000$  
- **D)** $1,54,800$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> After Year 1 $= 1,20,000 \times 1.10 = 1,32,000$.  
> After Year 2 $= 1,32,000 \times 1.20 = \mathbf{1,58,400}$.  
> *(Or effective increase $= 10 + 20 + \frac{200}{100} = 32\% \implies 1,20,000 \times 1.32 = 1,58,400$)*.

### Q17.
A machine was purchased for ₹$80,000$. Its value depreciates at a rate of $10\%$ per annum. What will be its value at the end of $3$ years?  
- **A)** ₹$56,000$  
- **B)** ₹$58,320$  
- **C)** ₹$64,800$  
- **D)** ₹$52,480$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Value after 3 years $= P \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^3 = 80,000 \times (0.9)^3$  
> $= 80,000 \times 0.729 = \mathbf{₹58,320}$.

---

### Q18.
The population of a town was $1,60,000$ two years ago. If it increased by $5\%$ in the first year and decreased by $5\%$ in the second year, what is the present population?  
- **A)** $1,60,000$  
- **B)** $1,59,600$  
- **C)** $1,58,400$  
- **D)** $1,60,400$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Net change $= +5 - 5 - \frac{25}{100} = -0.25\%$.  
> Present population $= 1,60,000 \times (1 - 0.0025) = 1,60,000 - 400 = \mathbf{1,59,600}$.

### Q19.
The value of a motorbike depreciates by $15\%$ each year. If its present value is ₹$36,125$, what was its value $2$ years ago?  
- **A)** ₹$48,000$  
- **B)** ₹$50,000$  
- **C)** ₹$45,000$  
- **D)** ₹$52,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Let initial value be $V$.  
> $V \times \left(1 - \frac{15}{100}\right)^2 = 36,125 \implies V \times (0.85)^2 = 36,125$  
> $V \times 0.7225 = 36,125 \implies V = \frac{36,125}{0.7225} = \mathbf{₹50,000}$.

### Q20.
The total population of a village was $12,000$. Over a year, the male population increased by $8\%$ and the female population increased by $5\%$, bringing the total population to $12,840$. What was the original female population?  
- **A)** $4,000$  
- **B)** $5,000$  
- **C)** $8,000$  
- **D)** $4,500$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Total increase $= 12,840 - 12,000 = 840$.  
> Let original males $= M$ and females $= F$.  
> $0.08 M + 0.05 F = 840$.  
> Also, $M + F = 12,000 \implies 0.05 M + 0.05 F = 600$.  
> Subtracting: $0.03 M = 240 \implies M = 8,000$.  
> $\implies F = 12,000 - 8,000 = \mathbf{4,000}$.

## Type 5: Exam Passing Marks & Score Percentages

### Q21.
A student scores $32\%$ marks in an exam and fails by $16$ marks. Another student scores $44\%$ marks and obtains $32$ marks more than the minimum passing marks. Find the maximum marks and the passing percentage.  
- **A)** $400 \text{ marks}, 36\%$  
- **B)** $500 \text{ marks}, 35\%$  
- **C)** $400 \text{ marks}, 40\%$  
- **D)** $450 \text{ marks}, 36\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Difference in percentage $= 44\% - 32\% = 12\%$.  
> Difference in marks $= 32 - (-16) = 48\text{ marks}$.  
> $12\% = 48 \implies 1\% = 4 \implies \text{Total Marks} = \mathbf{400}$.  
> Passing marks $= 32\% \text{ of } 400 + 16 = 128 + 16 = 144$.  
> Passing percentage $= \left(\frac{144}{400}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{36\%}$.

- **D)** $650$

In an examination, $A$ secured $10\%$ more marks than $B$, $B$ secured $20\%$ more than $C$, and $C$ secured $25\%$ less than $D$. If $D$ secured $400$ marks, find the marks secured by $A$.

> $A = 360 \times (1 + 0.10) = 360 \times 1.10 = \mathbf{396}$.

### Q25.
In a competitive test, there are three sections: Quant (100 marks), Verbal (100 marks), and Reasoning (150 marks). A student scored $60\%$ in Quant and $70\%$ in Verbal. What percentage must he score in Reasoning to secure an overall $70\%$ in the test?  
- **A)** $75\%$  
- **B)** $76.67\%$  
- **C)** $80\%$  
- **D)** $72.5\%$

> Required percentage $= \left(\frac{115}{150}\right) \times 100 = \frac{230}{3} = \mathbf{76.67\%} \text{ (or } 76\frac{2}{3}\%\text{)}$.

## Type 6: Income, Expenditure & Savings

### Q26.
Rajesh spends $20\%$ of his monthly income on house rent, $30\%$ of the **remaining** on food, and $50\%$ of the **balance** on personal expenses. If he is left with a saving of ₹$8,400$, find his total monthly income.  
- **A)** ₹$28,000$  
- **B)** ₹$30,000$  
- **C)** ₹$32,000$  
- **D)** ₹$35,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Let Income be $I$.  
> Remaining after rent $= I \times 0.80$.  
> Remaining after food $= I \times 0.80 \times (1 - 0.30) = I \times 0.56$.  
> Remaining after personal expenses $= I \times 0.56 \times (1 - 0.50) = I \times 0.28$.  
> Given $0.28 I = 8,400 \implies I = \frac{8,400}{0.28} = \mathbf{₹30,000}$.

### Q27.
Priya spends $75\%$ of her income. If her income increases by $20\%$ and her expenditure increases by $10\%$, find the percentage increase in her savings.  
- **A)** $40\%$  
- **B)** $45\%$  
- **C)** $50\%$  
- **D)** $60\%$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Let Initial Income $= 100 \implies \text{Expenditure} = 75, \text{Savings} = 25$.  
> New Income $= 100 \times 1.20 = 120$.  
> New Expenditure $= 75 \times 1.10 = 82.5$.  
> New Savings $= 120 - 82.5 = 37.5$.  
> Increase in Savings $= 37.5 - 25 = 12.5$.  
> Percentage Increase in Savings $= \left(\frac{12.5}{25}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{50\%}$.

### Q28.
A man spends $60\%$ of his income. If his income increases by $15\%$ and his expenditure also increases by $15\%$, by what percentage do his savings increase?  
- **A)** $10\%$  
- **B)** $12.5\%$  
- **C)** $15\%$  
- **D)** $30\%$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> **Key Concept**: When both income and expenditure increase by the same rate $x\%$, savings must also increase by the exact same rate $x\%$.  
> Verification: Let Income $= 100 \implies \text{Exp} = 60, \text{Sav} = 40$.  
> New Income $= 115$, New Exp $= 69 \implies \text{New Sav} = 115 - 69 = 46$.  
> Growth in Savings $= \frac{46 - 40}{40} \times 100 = \frac{6}{40} \times 100 = \mathbf{15\%}$.

### Q29.
An executive spends $12\%$ on rent, $22\%$ on grocery, $15\%$ on children's education, and $8\%$ on commute (all percentages of total income). If he saves ₹$21,500$, what is his total monthly income?  
- **A)** ₹$45,000$  
- **B)** ₹$50,000$  
- **C)** ₹$52,000$  
- **D)** ₹$48,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Total percentage spent $= 12\% + 22\% + 15\% + 8\% = 57\%$.  
> Percentage saved $= 100\% - 57\% = 43\%$.  
> $43\% = 21,500 \implies 1\% = \frac{21,500}{43} = 500$.  
> Total Income $(100\%) = 500 \times 100 = \mathbf{₹50,000}$.

### Q30.
The ratio of income to expenditure of a person is $8 : 5$. If his income increases by $25\%$ and his expenditure increases by $20\%$, what is the percentage increase in his savings?  
- **A)** $25\%$  
- **B)** $30\%$  
- **C)** $33.33\%$  
- **D)** $35\%$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Let Income $= 80$, Expenditure $= 50 \implies \text{Savings} = 80 - 50 = 30$.  
> New Income $= 80 \times 1.25 = 100$.  
> New Expenditure $= 50 \times 1.20 = 60$.  
> New Savings $= 100 - 60 = 40$.  
> Increase in Savings $= 40 - 30 = 10$.  
> $\% \text{ increase} = \left(\frac{10}{30}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{33.33\%} \text{ (or } 33\frac{1}{3}\%\text{)}$.

## Type 7: Election Problems (Voter Turnout & Margins)

### Q31.
In a direct election between two candidates, the winning candidate polled $58\%$ of the valid votes and defeated the other candidate by a majority of $3,840$ votes. What was the total number of valid votes polled?  
- **A)** $20,000$  
- **B)** $24,000$  
- **C)** $25,000$  
- **D)** $28,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Winner $= 58\% \implies \text{Loser} = 100\% - 58\% = 42\%$.  
> Margin $= 58\% - 42\% = 16\%$.  
> $16\% = 3,840 \implies 1\% = 240$.  
> Total valid votes $(100\%) = 240 \times 100 = \mathbf{24,000}$.

---

### Q32.
In an election between two candidates, $10\%$ of the voters on the list did not cast their vote, and $10\%$ of the votes cast were declared invalid. The winning candidate secured $54\%$ of the valid votes and won by $1,620$ votes. What was the total number of voters enrolled on the voter list?  
- **A)** $22,500$  
- **B)** $24,000$  
- **C)** $25,000$  
- **D)** $30,000$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> Let total enrolled voters be $V$.  
> Votes cast $= 0.90 V$.  
> Valid votes $= 0.90 V \times 0.90 = 0.81 V$.  
> Winner got $54\%$ of valid votes, Loser got $46\%$ of valid votes.  
> Margin $= 54\% - 46\% = 8\%$ of valid votes.  
> Margin in terms of $V = 0.08 \times 0.81 V = 0.0648 V$.  
> Given $0.0648 V = 1,620 \implies V = \frac{1,620}{0.0648} = \mathbf{25,000}$.

---

### Q33.
In an election, $20\%$ of the voters did not vote, and $120$ votes were declared invalid. The winner secured $41\%$ of the **total enrolled voters on the list** and won by $200$ votes. Find the total number of enrolled voters on the list.  
- **A)** $3,600$  
- **B)** $4,000$  
- **C)** $4,200$  
- **D)** $4,500$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Let total enrolled voters $= V$.  
> Votes polled $= 0.80 V$.  
> Valid votes $= 0.80 V - 120$.  
> Winner's votes $= 41\% \text{ of } V = 0.41 V$.  
> Loser's votes $= \text{Valid votes} - \text{Winner} = (0.80 V - 120) - 0.41 V = 0.39 V - 120$.  
> Difference (Margin) $= 0.41 V - (0.39 V - 120) = 0.02 V + 120$.  
> Given: $0.02 V + 120 = 200 \implies 0.02 V = 80 \implies V = \frac{80}{0.02} = \mathbf{4,000}$.

### Q34.
In an election, $8\%$ of the voters did not cast their votes. The winning candidate obtained $48\%$ of the **total voters on the roll** and won by $1,100$ votes over the only other candidate. What was the total number of voters on the roll?  
- **A)** $25,000$  
- **B)** $27,500$  
- **C)** $28,000$  
- **D)** $30,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Let total roll $= V$.  
> Total votes cast $= 100\% - 8\% = 92\% = 0.92 V$.  
> Winner $= 48\% \text{ of } V = 0.48 V$.  
> Loser $= 0.92 V - 0.48 V = 0.44 V$ (which is $44\%$ of $V$).  
> Margin $= 0.48 V - 0.44 V = 0.04 V = 4\% \text{ of } V$.  
> $4\% = 1,100 \implies V = \frac{1,100}{4} \times 100 = \mathbf{27,500}$.

### Q35.
In a three-candidate election, Candidate A received $40\%$ of the total votes, Candidate B received $36\%$, and Candidate C received the remaining $36,000$ votes. How many votes did the winning candidate receive?  
- **A)** $54,000$  
- **B)** $60,000$  
- **C)** $64,000$  
- **D)** $72,000$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Percentage for Candidate C $= 100\% - (40\% + 36\%) = 24\%$.  
> Given $24\% = 36,000 \implies 1\% = \frac{36,000}{24} = 1,500$.  
> The winner is Candidate A ($40\%$):  
> Votes for A $= 40 \times 1,500 = \mathbf{60,000}$.

## Type 8: Venn Diagrams & Set-Based Percentage Problems

### Q36.
In a college, $65\%$ of students play Cricket, $45\%$ play Football, and $25\%$ play both sports. What percentage of students play neither sport?  
- **A)** $10\%$  
- **B)** $15\%$  
- **C)** $20\%$  
- **D)** $25\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Total playing at least one sport:  
> $n(C \cup F) = n(C) + n(F) - n(C \cap F) = 65\% + 45\% - 25\% = 85\%$.  
> Playing neither sport $= 100\% - 85\% = \mathbf{15\%}$.

### Q37.
In an examination, $70\%$ candidates passed in English, $80\%$ passed in Mathematics, and $10\%$ failed in both subjects. If $144$ candidates passed in both subjects, find the total number of candidates.  
- **A)** $200$  
- **B)** $240$  
- **C)** $280$  
- **D)** $300$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Failed in both $= 10\% \implies$ Passed in at least one subject $= 100\% - 10\% = 90\%$.  
> We know: $n(E \cup M) = n(E) + n(M) - n(E \cap M)$  
> $90\% = 70\% + 80\% - n(E \cap M) \implies n(E \cap M) = 150\% - 90\% = 60\%$.  
> Given $60\% = 144 \implies \text{Total candidates} = \frac{144}{60} \times 100 = \mathbf{240}$.

### Q38.
In an IT company, $55\%$ of employees drink Tea, $60\%$ drink Coffee, and $30\%$ drink both. What percentage of employees drink **only** Tea?  
- **A)** $25\%$  
- **B)** $30\%$  
- **C)** $35\%$  
- **D)** $20\%$  

> **Solution: (A)**  
> Percentage who drink only Tea $= n(\text{Tea}) - n(\text{Tea and Coffee}) = 55\% - 30\% = \mathbf{25\%}$.  
> *(Similarly, only Coffee $= 60\% - 30\% = 30\%$)*.

### Q39.
In an examination, $42\%$ of candidates failed in Physics, $52\%$ failed in Chemistry, and $17\%$ failed in both subjects. What percentage of candidates passed in both subjects?  
- **A)** $21\%$  
- **B)** $23\%$  
- **C)** $25\%$  
- **D)** $27\%$  

> **Solution: (B)**  
> Failed in at least one subject:  
> $n(P_{\text{fail}} \cup C_{\text{fail}}) = 42\% + 52\% - 17\% = 77\%$.  
> Passed in both subjects $= 100\% - 77\% = \mathbf{23\%}$.

### Q40.
In a consumer survey of $500$ people, $64\%$ like Brand A and $56\%$ like Brand B. What is the **minimum** number of consumers who must like both brands?  
- **A)** $80$  
- **B)** $90$  
- **C)** $100$  
- **D)** $120$  

> **Solution: (C)**  
> For the overlap to be minimum, the union must be maximum $(\le 100\%)$.  
> $\text{Min } \% \text{ overlap} = 64\% + 56\% - 100\% = 20\%$.  
> Minimum number of consumers $= 20\% \text{ of } 500 = \mathbf{100}$.
